The budget of Britain's police force in one area for 2017 and 2018 is shown in the table and the two diagrams. They state the sources of the money and how it was spent. 
The National Government has contributed 175.5m pounds to the police budget in 2017 which is almost two times more than the amounts from local taxes and other sources - 91.2m and 38m pounds respectively. The budget spending is split into three categories: salaries for officers and staff, technology, and buildings and transport. 75% of all money is spent on salaries followed by buildings and transport at 17%. The least is spent on technology at 8% of the total budget.
In 2018 there has been a surge of 13.9m pounds in the total yearly budget mostly due to increased gains from local taxes. In terms of spending, salaries have plummeted by 6 percent while spending for buildings and transport remain stationary. The amount spent on technologies has increased from 8 to 14 percent.
Overall the comparisons give us insight into the increasing budget of the this area's police and the shifts of spending towards technology.








I believe the main factors which contribute to the drastic increase in time spent on smartphones by children are the engineering of apps to be addicting and the absence of parental control. 
Instead of spending time outside and playing with one's peers, many children have grown inseperable from their phones which is detrimental to their development. The first major factor which has contributed to this digital addiction is the financial motivation of mobile applications to keep the individual engaged for longer periods. Time spent engaging with an app corresponds to increases in profits from ads and higher amounts of in-app purchases. Multiple lawsuits have started against major mobile applications like Facebook and Instagram claiming they are both aware and negligent of the negative effects which their recommendation systems have on children.
However, such apps can start causing negatives only when kids are allowed to use them without limits. Recent studies have shown that there is a correlation between weekly time spent on phones and attention deficits when studying. Prevenetion enforced by parents is crucial in order to reduce the negative impact that smartphones have on the development of their children. Moreover, they should take the time to learn about parental controls in smartphones - these are tools which can be used to tailor the way a child uses the mobile device by disabling certain apps and setting usage limits.
In conclusion, the extensive use of smartphones has a negative effect on the younger generation which must be overcome by the joint efforts of parents and digital well-being tools.
