The two plans shown provide an overview of how Porth Harbour looked in 2000 and today. 

The plan from 2000 shows that the harbour had two car parks and showers and toilets right next to the main road on the west side. Next to them there is a Marina where private yachts are stored and a dedicated zone for the fishing boats. In the north there is a dock for passenger feries and an arrow pointing to a public beach situated even further north. The south road which enters the harbour leads to the lifeboat. Next to it, on the south-east side there is a disused castle and a public beach.
 
The Porth Harbour today has undergone significant changes since 2000. The storages for yachts and fishing boats have been swapped and a new dock for ferries has been constructed. The car parks have not changed, but a new showers and toilets facility has been built between them. Furthermore, cafes and shops have been constructed next to the lifeboat. The castle has been repurposed into a hotel and the south road has been extended to reach it. Additionally, the road leading to the south car park has been moved so now cars enter it directly from the main road on the west. Not only was the castle repurposed, but also the beach which was right next to it has been converted from a public to a private one, as part of the hotel. However, the public beach in the very north has remained the same.

In conclusion, there has been a number of changes in the harbour since 2000. These include the repurposing of buildings such as the castle, the construction of new ones such as the cafes and shops, and expansion of existing ones like the docks.






Multiple studies have been conducted to better understand how productivity of workers changes when the workweek is shorter. Companies have already began testing such methods in order to boost performance and increase morale.

Throughout history the working week has experienced multiple reductions in duration. Since the 1800 the working week has been halved from 80 hours to the current standard of 40 hours per week. I believe that decreasing the duration further will improve people's lives without affecting negatively the throughput of companies. Studies have shown that productivity increases when workers are well rested. Furthermore, significantly fewer life-threatening incidents, which are costly for both the worker and the company, happen when working fewer hours. Having a longer weekend can also reduce the strain on infrastructure and reduce prices of leisure activities as demand would be more spread out. 

Sceptics of the reduction of the workweek state that education of the youth would be negatively affected. I agree that as science advances, students will naturally need more time to grasp it. Exceptions can be made for high school and university education and a possible solution can be to shift when is the third leisure day for teachers. Also, this solution is applicable when concerns are raised about the services offered by professionals who are few in number such as surgeons. 
   
Overall, I believe that having a longer weekend will increase productivity. Problems associated with providing a good education and time critical services can be addressed through different measures such as introducing flexibility about which specific day people take off.
