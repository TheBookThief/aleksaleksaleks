The bar chart depicts the number of travels to and from school based on the trasnportation method used in one country. Data for the years 1990 and 2010 is shown, including only students aged between 5 and 12.

The dominant mode of transportation in 1990 was walking with more than 12 million trips per year. Taking the bus was the second most used means of transport at approximately 7 million rides, followed by cycling and the combination of walking and bus which sit at around 6 million trips for the year. The lowest used means of transport was being a car passenger at 4 million rides to and from school.

Data for 2010 shows a stark contrast, having almost three times more trips in a car at roughly 11 million. Meanwhile, walking saw a sharp decline falling to 6 million trips. Fewer than 6 million trips were by bus and around 3 million included the combination of walking and bus. Simultaneously cycling became the least popular mode of transportation at around 2 million rides.

Ultimately, both time periods had a highly dominant form of transportation for school travel. A decline in total number of school trips is seen in 2010 in comparison to 1990. Moreover, four out of the five modes of transport showcased experienced a reduction in utilization in the later period.






Advancements in agriculture, medicine, and technology have drastically increased the quality of life for the majority of people. I believe these factors will continue improving the average standard of people's health.

In the last decade the average lifespan has continously increased in almost all countries with no ongoing military conflicts. One of the commonly cited explanations is the diversification and increase in quality of food. Innovations in agriculture and trasnportation have brought down prices of healthy food. For example, the increase in demand for avocados, which contain various vitamins, was met through optimizations of marine transport routes. This led to the avocado losing its status of an exotic product, lowering its price, and making it available for ordinary consumers. Furthermore, innovation in farming technology has drastically increased yields which further lowers the price.

There is no indication that the standard of people's health will start declining. However, sceptics claim that innovations will provide only short-term benefits. With regards to agriculture they believe that the development of stronger pesticides will poison our food, and advances in medicine will force diseases to evolve, which will make them harder to overcome. I agree there is some truth to those concerns, but in the case of pesticides problems can be overcome through responsible legislation and regulation of compounds used to treat food. The concerns for drug development are legitimate but my belief is that the solution is to focus even more efforts in overcoming diseases rather than stopping progress.

Ultimately, I believe that human innovation will continue improving the lives of people and increase their lifespans as current trends suggest.
